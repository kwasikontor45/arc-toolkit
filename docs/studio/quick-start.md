# Small Studio: PC + iPhone starter path

This is a deliberately small, on-demand setup for learning beats, editing sound, DJ transitions, karaoke preparation, and simple event playback. It uses software already on this workstation. It does not install a sound server, cloud account, media library, or background service.

## What is ready on the PC

| App | Installed version | Best first use |
|---|---:|---|
| LMMS | 1.2.2 Flatpak | Make original beats and arrangements. Open the existing `ref/Reborn_Practice_Beat.mmp` and follow `ref/Reborn_LMMS_Baby_Steps.md`. LMMS is not a live DJ mixer and does not record a microphone. |
| Audacity | 3.7.8 Flatpak | Record a voice, trim/edit audio, balance a small set, make fades, and export a finished program file. Keep the editable project as well as the exported copy. |
| Mixxx | 2.5.6 system package | Practice two-deck mixing, beat matching, transitions, and cue points. Start with a few legally obtained local tracks; no controller is required to learn the basics with mouse and keyboard. |

Open these from **Khaos Lab → Studio**. The Markdown guides open in ReText 8.1.0 preview (also the default `.md` handler); apps launch only when clicked. Apostrophe was removed, along with its now-unused GNOME 49 runtime. Keep the first session simple: one app at a time, a few tracks, and no extra plugins.

## First practice session

1. **Create:** open the Reborn practice beat in LMMS. Change one drum step or tempo, then use **Save As** to make your own copy. Export a short WAV or OGG when ready.
2. **Edit:** open Audacity. Import the exported beat or a recording you own/have permission to use. Trim the start and end, add a gentle fade, listen at a comfortable level, and export a new WAV for quality or MP3 for a smaller playback copy.
3. **Mix:** open Mixxx and load two local tracks. Practice starting the next track, matching its tempo by ear, and moving the crossfader slowly. Save cue points only after the transition feels comfortable.
4. **Prepare an event:** fill in `event-cue-sheet-template.md`. Test the whole playlist in order, including its opening, microphone moments, ending, and a backup copy before the day.

For a first baby shower or small gathering, a pre-tested playlist or one continuous Audacity export is more dependable than improvising. Mixxx is useful when you want live transitions. Use local files that you own or are licensed for the event; a streaming-service subscription may not grant public-performance rights.

## iPhone SE (3rd generation)

GarageBand for iOS is a light starting point for sketching beats, loops, and short recordings on the phone. Apple’s [GarageBand overview](https://www.apple.com/ios/garageband/) covers its touch instruments and Live Loops. The built-in microphone and speaker are fine for private ideas and checking a mix; they are not a microphone or PA for an audience. AirDrop or another trusted local transfer can move your own exported practice file between iPhone and PC. No workstation remote-control app is needed for this starter setup.

## What is missing for real event work

You said you currently have no microphone, speakers, or lights. The phone and laptop outputs are for practice, not for filling a room. Add gear only when you know the room size and budget.

| Need | Minimum useful item | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hear the mix privately | Wired, closed-back headphones | Lets you pre-listen and cue the next track; wired avoids Bluetooth delay. |
| Play for a room | Powered PA speaker; stereo pair for music where practical | Laptop/phone speakers are too quiet for an audience. Choose output and coverage for the room. |
| DJ control | Optional Mixxx-supported USB controller with headphone cue output | The mouse works for learning. A mapped controller makes hands-on transitions easier. Check Mixxx’s hardware guide before buying. |
| Voice/karaoke | Dynamic handheld microphone, XLR cable, and a speaker or compact mixer with a mic input | Prevents feedback surprises and provides the correct input path. Test mic level and placement before guests arrive. |
| Small event reliability | Correct audio cables/adapters, safe power strip, speaker stands, and a tested backup playback device | Prevents last-minute connector and playback failures. Keep cables out of walkways. |
| Lighting (later) | A pair of DMX-capable LED wash lights, suitable stands/cables, and a compatible controller/interface | Start with simple static color and brightness; learn power and DMX addressing before programming effects. |

Skip smoke/haze equipment at the beginner stage. It can trigger alarms and venue restrictions; use it only with venue approval, safe ventilation, and the fire-safety plan. Never disable or cover alarms.

## Keep it light and safe

- Keep Mixxx, Audacity, and LMMS closed when not in use. They have no required always-on service here.
- Do not raise system volume to compensate for missing PA gear. Keep the master meter out of the red and test quietly first.
- Prefer WAV for editing and a tested MP3/WAV copy for playback. Keep project files so edits remain possible.
- Keep a second copy of the final event playlist on a separate device before the event.
- Learn the room, speaker placement, microphone gain, and cable routing before adding lights or effects.
